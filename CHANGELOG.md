# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Weekly limits only.** Claude plans no longer have a 5-hour limit, so none is drawn, forecast or explained: Home, the line above the prompt, alerts, the pace warning, the model adviser, Usage, the status tool, the phone lobby and the report card are about the weekly limit and any model's own weekly limit. A session that still reports another kind of limit lists it as a limit of its own, after the weekly ones and without a forecast. Settings and the description of *Alert at* say weekly.
- **The report card says something new under the figure.** Under "82% of my weekly limit" it now gives where the limit is headed (when it runs out, or the pace to the reset) instead of repeating the figure as "work worth 82% of the limit"; the models bar is one continuous rounded bar, with a small model's segment kept visible inside it.
- **Alerts that feel alive.** When this chat starts waiting on you (a permission, a question, or a finished answer waiting for your next message) one alert goes out for that wait, by default, even with Notifications off (*Alert when this chat waits for you*). It uses Claude Code's own notification channel, falling back to the computer's notification centre only where that shows no words, and plays a chime through the engine's own `$.audio.play` (no more `afplay`/`paplay` unless the engine has no player). *Say alerts aloud* speaks a few words with `$.audio.speak`. The alert is rate-limited like every other (once per wait, four in ten minutes), says who is waiting and never the command, and a line under an open question says what else waits.
- **The next prompt, suggested.** Near compaction the dim suggestion offers `/compact` with the plan kept, and after a limit warning `/tobebestme-handoff`, once per crossing or window; Tab takes it, and nothing is typed for you (*Suggest the next prompt*).
- **A status tool for Claude.** `mcp__tobebestme__status` (read-only, allowed without a prompt) answers where limits, context, cost, waiting chats and running workflows stand, so you can just ask Claude.
- **Pull requests and CI in Needs you.** With the GitHub CLI installed and signed in, your open pull requests that need you appear: review requested, changes requested, checks failing, merge conflicts, ready to merge; open or copy the link. Read in the background at most every five minutes with `gh`'s own read queries; with no `gh` or no sign-in Settings says why (*Pull requests and CI in Needs you*).
- **A weekly report card.** On Usage: the week as one image (figure, days by project, days used and streak, top projects, models, workflows and savings), saved as an SVG and, where a converter exists, a PNG, with a short text to paste. Project names can be hidden first.
- **The phone gets a lobby.** On the mobile app, what waits for you with the replies that exist, the tightest limit with its run-out, and running workflows.

- **The week calendar fills its row in the app.** The desktop app drew it at the browser's default 300px, squashing its labels and putting the bars under the wrong days. It now has its own size, and the day buttons share its width. The test renderer now sizes drawings the way the app does, so the check catches this.
- **Limit names read as phrases**: "of your weekly limit, all models", not "of your weekly · all models".
- **Every tab opens on its one answer.** This session leads with its cost and the context window against the point Claude Code compacts, with a ghost for the next turns and a bar per turn, and says whether it will make it. Usage leads with the forecast of the limit that matters most and where the week went (days by project, projects, models). Workflows leads with what is running as runways of cost against limit, or the month's cost and the part of it that could have been saved. Skills leads with the listing as a runway against its room, and Settings with how much is on autopilot and whether everything it reads could be read.
- **Pick up folds its rows.** The latest project is drawn whole and the others are a line each until opened, as Needs you does; Skill ideas shows three.
- **Light mode is tested.** Words and marks in drawings have light-theme colours (the white "Now", grey labels, amber and red), words drawn as text use colours that read on both panes, and the e2e suite checks the real states on a light pane for contrast.
- **The week's first day starts when the window did.** Its bar no longer counts that morning, from the hours the records now keep.
- **Privacy wording matches what it does.** Two footnotes said "Nothing leaves your computer". They now say reading Claude Code's records sends nothing anywhere, and SECURITY.md lists the lines *Tell Claude where the budget stands* adds to a prompt when a limit is close, and the handoff-note pointer after a compaction.
- **One number, one name.** The same limit now reads the same everywhere: spans in alerts and headlines use the format of Home ("32 h 38 min", not "a day"), models are "Opus 5.5" rather than `opus-5-5`, the cheaper model is capitalised ("Helpers now use Haiku"), and a pace is labelled as the projection at the reset. Today, in every session, is never below this session's own figure, and the band names them apart ("This session … of the weekly limit · Today, all sessions: …"). Free space on This session is everything not in use, with the part kept for compaction shown as part of it.
- **Weeks, not 300%.** A workflow cost beyond the whole weekly limit says "about 3 weeks’ worth", with "estimated at list prices"; savings lead with the share of the limit and give dollars second. The first saving has the one filled button and the rest are quiet text. The spending limit on a running workflow is "Spending limit, then hold new agents", so it no longer shares a name with the Pause new agents button, and a timeline of agents only a few seconds old is not drawn as two specks.
- **Narrow panes are designed.** Values stack under their labels, tables drop bars before numbers, Open sessions wrap their names and show the project (the branch and path are in the hover card), the permission label sits on its own line without repeating itself, and the line above the prompt keeps its headline and button on a phone.
- **Quieter secondary buttons, clearer empty states.** Copy resume command, Set aside, Copy run ID and Search again are quiet text; "Archive session…" is no longer dimmed; "Use Haiku for helpers" and "Switch this conversation to Sonnet" no longer compete with different advice. Empty Workflows is one column with a Start a workflow button, empty Skills says what a skill is and offers Build your first skill, empty Usage has no card of nothing, and an unreadable records folder is drawn as an error with a next step. Get more from Claude says what the count is.
- **Light mode.** Orange text is a deeper orange (4.5:1), the runway's track, strand and posts are firmer, the Now playhead turns dark on white, sections have a stronger fill, and the grey of helper text and chips holds 4.5:1.
- **Home tells the week as one story.** One drawing carries the weekly figure and the forecast, each day's work by project, the line of use so far, the dashed line where the pace leads, the run-out and the hours without Claude before the reset. The most urgent chat waiting on you is a card of its own, the rest a compact list. Savings are drawn: as run against with the ideas applied, with the points back, and each idea's share as a bar.
- **Workflow suggestions come only from what you type.** An agent's report or a task notification that read like a job was offered back as a request.
- **Buttons do the job in the pane, not in the conversation.** Fix description…, Make skills manual-only…, Give skills more room… and Make it a rule… used to put a request in the prompt box for Claude. Now the pane works the change out itself (the helper model writes a skill's new description), shows it on its tab as now and after, and writes the file only on Save change; a file edited meanwhile is never written over. Improve it rewrites a skill draft in place, with Undo. Manage MCP servers, memory, plugins and skills run their command directly. Starting a workflow, resuming one, a long run and Try /name still go to the conversation, as they hand Claude a job to do.
- **Checked against the Claude app's own renderer.** The app refuses a drawing with blank words, so the field of colour behind each tab's head was never drawn there; it is gone, and the glow now lives inside each hero figure. Interactive meters and bar charts given only a height were drawn in a 300px frame; every drawing now carries its size and the stretched ones fill their row. Entrances no longer replay on the redraw every turn brings: each plays once when its tab opens and stays still after. The e2e renderer now follows the app's rules (blank words draw nothing, frames and images sized as the app sizes them, no stacking order), and a new check runs every interactive drawing through the same DOMPurify scrub the app uses.
- **The desktop pane gets a cinematic pass** (the terminal is unchanged). Big figures are gradients in each tab's colours (amber or red once a limit is past its alert), with a soft bloom of their own colours under the digits. Runways sweep in and carry a moving sheen on the projected ghost, the run-out marker pulses and the stretch after it is hatched; the week's bars spring up with tokens falling into them. Every animation stops under reduced motion. Tests cover the markup, the contrast of the gradients and the reduced-motion switch.

## 1.4.0

- **Your model choice wins.** A model you switch to with `/model` is no longer overridden by Always mode, a budget or a dashboard choice made earlier: the session stays on it and says so. Before, Always silently sent every request, the main conversation too, to the cheaper model, so a switch seemed to do nothing. While Always is sending this conversation to the cheaper model, the dashboard now says so, with a one-press **Turn Always off**.
- **Workflows end on time.** A run is now seen to finish with the pane closed, so the line above the prompt stops saying "running" and the "workflow finished" notification goes out.
- **Notifications go out once per computer.** Each alert has a stable key and a record every session shares, with at most four notifications in any ten minutes. They are sent in the background, so a sound never holds up the pane.
- **Suggested workflows.** A request that looks like a job for a workflow is suggested as one, with the kind, its stages and the model for each.
- **Run spending limits and the weekly-limit share.** A workflow can pause new agents past a spending limit. Costs are also shown as a share of your weekly limit, for plans that bill that way.
- **Ways to spend less** come from your own finished runs, as ranges, with a follow-up once you adopt one.
- **Narrow panes.** Tables drop the columns that don't fit, the usage forecast keeps 11px labels on a phone-width pane, and table cells keep a gutter.
- **Clearer actions.** Buttons say what they do ("Review workflow", "Refresh runs", "Copy run ID"), and the desktop tab bar is a segmented control, so the one primary button per view stands out.
- **Runways instead of rings.** The three concentric usage rings, which read as a watch face, are replaced by a runway per item: the fill is what is used, a strand under it is how much of the window has passed, and a limit that would run out early shows its run-out and the dry stretch to the reset. It is on the This session tiles, the Home limit tile and the line above the prompt, and in block characters on the terminal. A design panel of four directions was judged on distinctiveness, clarity and pace; this one won.
- **Animated chart tooltips** on the usage forecast. They fade in over 150 ms, and appear at once with reduced motion set.
- **Tamper evidence.** Releases ship SHA-256 checksums and build provenance. ToBeBestMe checks its own files against its release once a day.
- **Security.** Untrusted text is cleaned before display, keys from other sessions are checked, session ids are quoted, the handoff note is treated as data, and the store is capped by size.
- **A calmer, structured layout.** Sections are a heading above a faint grouped fill instead of bordered cards with icon tiles; the tabs sit in one segmented track; Home leads with the tightest limit as a full-width runway and one strip of figures; the most pressing waiting chat is drawn whole and the rest in one line each; on a wide pane Home, This session, Usage, Workflows, Context and Settings are two columns, with figures and controls beside the work. One primary button per view is enforced.
- **Fewer tabs.** Context is part of This session now (six tabs, and `/tobebestme context` still opens it). Each control lives in one place (budgets on Usage, the workflow check on Workflows). A section's explaining line shows under Show details, so the page stays quiet; the lines that carry a fact (how old a plan reading is, what notifications can show) stay. Usage draws a limit as the same runway as Home and the line above the prompt, with the history chart under Show details. The line above the prompt is two lines: the headline and its cost, then the figures.
- **Get more from Claude.** Home suggests up to three things at a time, each one press away, and puts one off for a month with Not now. They come from your own settings and records, never a fixed list: what the records show costing you (MCP servers re-sent with every request, skills that never run) comes first, then habits you could start (notifications, a skill for a prompt you repeat, a memory file, a first workflow, a spending guard). The card says how many habits are in place and goes away when there is nothing left to suggest.
- **A welcome tour.** The first time, four steps show the real thing and not a picture of it: the line above the prompt as it is now, what waits for you, your tightest limit laid out on its calendar, and the habits. It waits while a chat needs an answer, and `/tobebestme tour` brings it back. What it remembers (the tour is done, which suggestions were put off) is one small record in the shared store, `habits:v1`.
- **The week on a calendar.** The limit that matters most is drawn large on Home and Usage: the window's days (or hours) along the bottom, Now over the playhead, and where it runs out and when it resets in words over their posts. The smaller runways keep their short form.
- **Ask me first is a real gate.** It asks in the chat's own question dialog, which a hook can wait on for as long as you take, and a workflow agent held by a pause waits in short process naps. Before, both gave way after ten seconds and the run started anyway.
- **Notifications.** One store record per event, a claim at a time, `--` before the words of a system notification, an encoded phone header for non-ASCII titles, and budget notices that name their amount.
- **Costs as a share of your weekly limit.** On a Claude plan what runs out is the limit, not dollars, so by default every cost (Home, Usage, Workflows, the line above the prompt, the status line and the line under each answer) shows as a share of the weekly limit, estimated from this week's work at list prices against how far the limit has moved. Usage › Show costs as switches between Automatic, Share of limit and Dollars. Budgets stay in dollars.
- **Every move lands where it says.** A new tab opens at its top instead of keeping the last tab's place on the page. A button for one thing scrolls it into view: Make the conversation smaller goes to Save and shrink, Open model adviser to the adviser, a budget link to the budgets. A question a button asks (Start fresh, Compact now, a reply) comes into view, and Start fresh or Compact now pressed outside This session opens This session, where the question is.
- **Colour that names things.** Each limit keeps one vivid colour wherever it is drawn (weekly blue, a model's own weekly limit pink, context cyan, budgets mint), and each of your six most used projects gets one too, worn in the week's bars, the projects list, the busiest project and Pick up. Amber (past your alert, or running out early) and red (nearly out) take over a limit's colour everywhere, so trouble looks the same on every tab and in the line above the prompt. Charts carry light-theme variants of the vivid colours.
- **Your week, by project, on the limit's calendar.** Under Home's runway, each day of the window is a bar stacked by project, so you can see which work filled the week; point at a day for its share and its busiest projects. The other limits sit under it as runways, so Home's hero holds every limit, and the strip of figures and the plan card are gone: today's figures are in the headline's line and the week's total beside Projects. Where there is no week to draw (no plan), the 14-day chart is stacked by project instead.
- **Nothing cut off at any width.** In the app, a long session title or branch name held its column open and pushed the right-hand column past the pane's edge, worst on a pane made full screen. Every box may now shrink below its longest line, and long text truncates. The e2e renderer had hidden this by letting every box shrink; it now sizes boxes as the app does, the checks run at 1920 pixels too, and the data has long titles and branch names in it. Interactive drawings (Home's calendar, Usage's forecast) now say their size: the app puts one in a frame of its own, and a frame given no size is a small default one with the drawing shrunk inside it. The e2e renderer draws such a frame as the app does, so a drawing without a size fails the checks.
- **Where the pace lands.** Every runway (Home, This session, Usage, the line above the prompt, and in blocks on the terminal) shows a faint ghost ahead of its fill: where the window's own pace lands by the reset. Home leads with the tightest limit as one large figure beside the headline, and under its name a sentence of forecast: on pace to end the week at about so much, or at this pace it runs out at such a time.
- **What a saving would do.** Each saving on Home (helpers on Haiku, half of Opus on Sonnet) says where the week's pace would land instead, from list prices, and its hover card draws the runway the week would have, with before and after in words. Show details lays it out in place.
- **The week is something to press.** Under Home's calendar each day is a button in its own column: press one and the other days step back, a line says what that day held, project by project, and Projects shows it. Press a project in the legend and it sits on the baseline in its colour every day while the rest go grey, and its row stands out in Projects. One press brings each back. The calendar now spans its column exactly whatever size the app's cells are.
- **What is working looks alive.** A workflow running, a session working now and a run in the line above the prompt have a dot that pulses softly, as the system marks a live activity; with reduced motion set it is still.
- **A better line above the prompt.** Each figure (context, the weekly limits, a run) takes an equal share of the row, its runway filling the room between its name and its percentage, so pace reads at a glance instead of in a 40-pixel sliver. The day's figure joins the headline's line. On a phone-width band the figures go two to a row.
- **Context against the window you set.** With `autoCompactWindow` set below the model's window (say 700k on a 1M model), context was shown as a share of the model's whole window, so it looked half full when compaction was near. It is now measured against the window compaction works to, everywhere: the pane, the line above the prompt, the status line, the line under each answer and the cheaper-model switch. The context tile says which window it measures against.
- **Smaller fixes.** A session the app has not measured shows "–" for its context, not "NaN%". Open sessions keeps room for titles on a narrow column. Pick up leaves out code and pasted scripts from what a session last said. A busiest-project line says it did that share of the week's work, as the other lines do, in the page's own unit.
- **Fixes from the pre-launch review.**
  - Routing to the cheaper model is paused only when it fails while the usual model answers (not when the connection is down), and the pause lasts half an hour, not the rest of the session.
  - Only a model switch you make (/model, the /config row, a picker) wins over Always; an automatic fallback or a resume doesn't.
  - A reply to a chat that ran in a terminal or an editor can no longer reach a Claude app session with the same folder and title.
  - The model adviser knows Fable and Mythos: with limits tight it suggests Opus, with one press to switch.
  - "Suggest more with Sonnet" now asks Sonnet when the account has no cheaper model.
  - A button that puts a command in the prompt box leaves words you were typing there, and says how to go on.
  - The pane closes with Esc however it was opened, from the line above the prompt too.
  - A handoff note written mid-conversation isn't pointed out to Claude at the next prompt; only one written before a compaction or a fresh start is.
  - Wins this session are counted as they happen, so clearing the activity list keeps them. Saved reports use this computer's time.
  - Set-aside chats, set-aside savings and put-off suggestions are merged with what other sessions stored, not written over. After a /clear the session follows its new id, and its record of which copy ran it goes when it ends. Spending is read sixteen records at a time, and only today's are read again. Transcripts dropped for room are not read again on every pass.
  - Skill names such as `toString` or `valueOf` are skills like any other, and `__proto__` or `constructor` from a record are left out.
  - The self-check reports a module added beside the released ones, and a manifest missing from a copy that had one. `SHA256SUMS` now covers README.md and SECURITY.md too.
  - SECURITY.md lists everything ToBeBestMe reads (settings files for two skill settings, a workflow's script, the environment variables it uses) and every prompt it sends.
  - The e2e sessions load a copy of the mod as it ships, the live test runs under a name of its own each time, the desktop checks measure against the pane's own edges, and the terminal checks confirm Show details draws the hover cards in place.
- **Tests.** 79 kit tests and an end-to-end suite. The suite draws every view at four widths and checks it, drives real terminal sessions, and runs one live workflow.
